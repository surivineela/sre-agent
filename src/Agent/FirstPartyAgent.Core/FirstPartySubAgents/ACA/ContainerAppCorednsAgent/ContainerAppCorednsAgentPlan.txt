### 🔹 Step 3: Remediation Guidance

> Note: ** Only suggest remediation steps that are applicable based on observed findings. Do not suggest general advice when no issues are found in the relevant diagnostics.**

If relevant issues are observed, suggest the corresponding bullet points below:

- ✅ **If custom DNS server issues are detected:**
  - Recommend validating health of custom DNS servers.

- ✅ **If repeated CoreDNS pod failures or degraded health is detected:**
  - Recommend restarting CoreDNS pods as a mitigation step.

- ✅ **If inconsistent DNS resolutions AND custom DNS servers are configured:**
  - Optionally suggest disabling fallback to Azure DNS for more consistent DNS behavior.

    To disable fallback:
    ```powershell
    Set-ManagedClusterCustomHelmValues `
        -Name <managed-cluster-name> `
        -UpgradeChart `
        -CustomHelmValues '{"removeAzureDnsFallbackForApps": "true"}' `
        -Region <region>
    ```

- ✅ **If `CheckIfDNSServerFailedToResolveDot` shows repeated failures:**
  - Recommend adding a wildcard `.` rule to DNS Forwarding Ruleset.

    To fix this when using **Azure DNS Private Resolver**:

    1. Go to your **DNS Forwarding Ruleset** in the Azure Portal.  
    2. Select the **Rules** tab → Click **Add**.  
    3. Fill in:
       - **Rule Name**: `Wildcard`
       - **Domain Name**: `.`  
       - **Rule State**: `Enabled`
       - **Target DNS Servers**: e.g., `168.63.129.16`

    📘 [Full guide here](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/dns/dns-private-resolver-get-started-portal#configure-a-dns-forwarding-ruleset)

  - Optionally suggest restarting CoreDNS pods to force the use of the updated custom DNS configuration.
